STEP 32 — Expression Patterning (Expression Families)

Goal

Implement the first structural analysis of the Semantic Dashboard.

This step moves beyond listing individual expression candidates and begins identifying recurring expression families.

The purpose is to reveal how multiple expressions share common structural patterns through lexical variation.

This is still descriptive.

Do NOT infer formulaicity, entrenchment, or fossilisation yet.


IMPORTANT

Do NOT modify:

- Discursive tab
- Lexical tab
- preprocessing
- tokenisation
- lemmatisation
- stoplists
- extraction logic introduced in Step 31

Do NOT add:

- collocation association measures
- MI
- logDice
- formulaicity scoring
- entrenchment
- fossilisation
- diachronic analysis

This step analyses expression structure only.


Scope

Apply ONLY to:

client/src/pages/Analysis.tsx

Focus ONLY on:

Section B — Expression Patterning

Do NOT modify Sections A, C, or D except where shared helper functions are genuinely required.


Analytical Object

Expression Family

Definition:

A set of recurring expressions sharing the same structural frame but differing by one or more lexical slots.


Implementation Steps

1.

Reuse the expression candidates already generated in Step 31.

Do NOT regenerate n-grams independently.

Expression Patterning should build directly upon the existing candidate list.


2.

Construct simple expression families.

For version 1:

Expressions belong to the same family when they differ in exactly one token position.

Examples:

my love
your love
his love
her love

↓

Family:

[Possessive] love

------------------------

good my lord
sweet my lord
dear my lord

↓

Family:

[Adjective] my lord

------------------------

lord jesus christ
lord king henry

↓

Family:

lord [X] [X]

Use a simple wildcard representation such as:

[X]


3.

For each family calculate:

- family pattern
- number of member expressions
- total family frequency
- most frequent member


4.

Sort families by:

1. total family frequency

then

2. number of member expressions


5.

Display Section B using TWO complementary views.


VIEW 1

Top Expression Families

Columns:

- Family Pattern
- Members
- Total Frequency
- Most Frequent Expression


VIEW 2

Family Members

Selecting a family reveals:

- every member expression
- frequency
- percentage within family

Use an expandable row or drill-down card.

Do NOT navigate away from the page.


6.

Add a simple horizontal frequency bar to the Family table.

Reuse the visual style introduced in Step 31A.


7.

Add CSV export.

Export:

- family
- member count
- total frequency
- representative expression


8.

Add summary block.

Include:

- total families
- average family size
- largest family
- most frequent family


9.

Add empty-state message:

"No recurring expression families were identified for the current selection."


10.

Terminology.

Use:

Expression Family

Family Pattern

Member Expression

Avoid:

Cluster

Constellation

Configuration

These belong to the Discursive tab.


Safety Constraints

The agent must NOT:

- modify LexicalTab
- modify DiscursiveTab
- alter expression extraction
- add formulaicity
- add conventionalisation metrics
- add new preprocessing
- change existing exports from Step 31

Only implement Section B.


Expected Result

After Step 32:

Section B becomes the first structural analysis of the Semantic Dashboard.

Researchers can:

without yet making interpretive claims about conventionalisation.
